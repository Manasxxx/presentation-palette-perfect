# UI Refactoring and Design Guide

This resource outlines a systematic approach to interface design that emphasizes logical principles over artistic intuition.

## Key Principles

The methodology follows a deliberate sequence: **start with core features** rather than navigation shells, **use low-fidelity grayscale work** to establish layout before considering aesthetics, and **establish restrictive design systems** for consistency across spacing, typography, and color.

## Essential Tactics

The guide references specialized resources covering:
- **Visual hierarchy**: Size, weight, contrast, and semantic meaning
- **Layout organization**: Grid systems, spacing scales, and information density
- **Typography**: Type scales, line-height, and font selection
- **Color systems**: HSL-based palettes with accessible contrast ratios
- **Refinement**: Light sources, imagery, and finishing details

## Practical Rules

Two core heuristics guide decision-making:

1. "If you can't decide between two options, you have too many choices" — constrain your palette, sizing, and spacing to predefined scales
2. Personality should drive stylistic choices (serious designs use serifs and formal language; friendly designs favor rounded sans-serifs and casual tone)

The approach prioritizes designing the straightforward "happy path" first, then iterating toward edge cases rather than anticipating complexity upfront.
